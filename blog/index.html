What's on the radar

  • Consumer sentiment at 51.0 — only 8% expect to get ahead

    Michigan sentiment plunged from 55.2 to 51.0 in early August. Expected business conditions sank 11% short-run, 17% long-run. Only 8% of consumers expect income to outpace inflation, down from 18% in December 2024. Year-ahead inflation expectations 4.3%. July retail sales fell 0.6%. The consumer is cracking.

  • Trump declares Hormuz will "soon" be US territory

    At a New York rally Aug 14, Trump vowed to declare the Strait of Hormuz US territory after defeating Iran. Iran's FM: "will remain Iranian." No peace talks planned. ADNOC vessels attacked in strait Aug 13-14. Bessent threatens "economic isolation like the world has never seen."

  • Nvidia's $500B AI financing push

    MOUs with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, KKR. Jensen Huang called compute an "investable asset class." AI capex heading to 2.8% of GDP by 2028, but only 17-20% of businesses report using AI. The gap between spending and adoption is widening.

  • Europe drought deepens — Rhine at record lows

    Rhine at 145-year low, Kaub gauge forecast to fall to single digits. Germany could lose 0.4% GDP — wiping out entire 2026 growth. Romania shut last Danube nuclear reactor. EU sweltering summer could cost €180B (1% of GDP). El Niño may extend warm conditions into spring 2027.

The Emergency Buffer Is Gone

15 August 2026

The SPR fell below 300M barrels for the first time since the 1980s. Two wars in four years drained 352M barrels. Energy Dept officials told GAO they're "holding the SPR together with Band-Aids." The reserve that was built to cushion a supply crisis is nearly gone, at the moment global supply is most constrained.

The Record High That Doesn't See the Economy

15 August 2026

The S&P 500 hit 7,798.99. The US lost 23,000 jobs. Participation at 61.4%. Retail sales fell. The index is not the economy, but the gap between them has never been wider — and the fiscal-monetary bind means neither the Fed nor the government can close it.

The Diesel Crisis Is Structural, Not Cyclical

14 August 2026

Russia's diesel exports crashed to 80K bpd. Crack spreads at record highs. Global refinery throughputs 5M bpd below year-ago. This is not a Hormuz blip — it is the convergence of Russian refinery destruction, aging global capacity, and structurally underestimated demand.

The Fed's Bind: Inflation Won't Break, and the Labor Market Already Has

14 August 2026

The FOMC voted 9-3 to hold — the most divided vote in years — with inflation still above target and the first payroll decline since the pandemic. Rates stay elevated because inflation won't break. The economy is slowing because labor already has. The government can't stimulate. This is where risk lives now.
