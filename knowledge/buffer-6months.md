# Buffer — six months

This is the long-term memory of the field. It holds what would change
someone's reading of the whole field, not just this week: the events that
actually mattered, and the longer theses and hypotheses that are playing out
over months. It is the editorial line — when the short-term buffer says what's
happening, this file says what it means and where it's heading.

It is bounded, but by significance rather than age. An entry stays as long as
it carries weight and goes when it doesn't — a thesis that played out, an
impact that faded, a shift that got superseded by something more important.
New impactful developments are welcome to displace older, weaker ones. Keep
it to the dozen or two things that genuinely shape the field.

Every entry carries the exact source URL it came from, and a hypothesis
records the observation it grew out of.

## Impactful events

- **U.S.-Israel war with Iran / Hormuz closure** — The defining risk event of 2026. Energy prices spiked in March (energy CPI +10.9% in one month), then eased as markets adjusted, but the strait remains effectively closed. IEA projects 1.8M bpd deficit this quarter — more than double prior estimate. Capital Economics warns of $120-140 oil by late September/early Q4 if flows stay restricted. Inventories depleting globally. The physical market is tightening faster than futures suggest. (Multiple sources, Aug 2026 — https://oilprice.com/, https://oilprice.com/Energy/Oil-Prices/Hormuz-Stalemate-Raises-Risk-of-120-Oil.html)

- **The global diesel crisis** — The real oil market squeeze is in refined products, not crude. Russia's diesel exports crashed to 80K bpd in early August, down from 1M bpd last year, as Ukrainian drone strikes cripple refineries. Global refinery throughputs ~5M bpd below year-ago. Atlantic Basin crack spreads at record highs. IEA projects global seaborne petroleum product trade down 3.8M bpd. Jefferies, Goldman, and BofA all flag diesel as the critical bottleneck. BofA: diesel will stay "tight, volatile, and expensive well into next year." (Aug 2026, https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Russias-Diesel-Exports-Crash-to-Multiyear-Low-amid-Tight-Global-Market.html, https://oilprice.com/Energy/Crude-Oil/Jefferies-Diesel-Cracks-Reveal-the-Real-Oil-Market-Squeeze.html)

- **OPEC and IEA slash demand outlooks** — IEA now expects 1.6M bpd demand destruction this year, 510K bpd below July expectations. OPEC cut 2026 growth to 580K bpd. Both agencies acknowledging that sustained high prices are destroying demand — but this is price-driven demand destruction, not supply relief. The market is in a price-destruction paradox: prices stay high because supply is constrained, but the longer they stay high, the more demand erodes. (Aug 2026, https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Oil-Prices-Fall-as-OPEC-and-IEA-Slash-2026-Demand-Outlooks.html)

- **Fed's hawkish pivot under Kevin Warsh** — Warsh took over as Fed chair in May 2026. The FOMC voted 9-3 to hold rates, with three dissenters wanting a hike. Markets now expect first hike in October or December 2026. Warsh previously argued AI would be disinflationary, but has adopted a more cautious tone. This is the most divided the Fed has been in years. (Aug 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/cpi-inflation-report-july-2026.html)

- **U.S. labor market cracking** — July saw the first nonfarm payrolls decline (-23K) after a string of weak months. Participation rate at 61.4%, lowest since 1976 outside Covid. Employment-to-population ratio 58.9%, lowest since May 2014. Immigration slowdown removing a key labor supply buffer. (Aug 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/jobs-report-july-2026.html)

- **U.S. fiscal deterioration** — FY2026 deficit ~$1.8T, already surpassing 2025. Debt servicing costs $1.17T on $39.9T national debt. Markets not pricing any rate cut for five years. The fiscal picture constrains whatever the Fed wants to do. (Aug 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/us-budget-deficit-surged-in-july-to-highest-level-since-march-2021.html)

- **AI capex supercycle meeting reality** — $581B U.S. AI spending in 2026 (1.8% GDP), heading to 2.8% by 2028. But only 17-20% of businesses report using AI. Costs are real (electricity +10%, DRAM +400%, software CPI +22.4%) while productivity gains remain elusive. The gap between frontier firms and average firms is widening. (Aug 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/ais-costly-buildout-complicates-the-feds-inflation-fight.html)

- **European drought as economic shock** — Rhine at 145-year low. Romania shut its last nuclear reactor, Danube at 90-year low. France power prices jumped 22% as heatwave trimmed nuclear output. Germany could lose 0.2% GDP in Q3. Climate change making this a recurring structural challenge, not a one-off. (Aug 2026, https://oilprice.com/, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/drought-rhine-danube-water-levels-economy.html)

- **Ukraine war energy escalation** — Ukraine striking deep into Russia: Gazprom Salavat refinery (200K bpd) hit, Novorossiysk grain terminals damaged. Russia's diesel exports crashed to 80K bpd. Russia importing Indian fuel. Global diesel supply crunch deepening. (Aug 2026, https://oilprice.com/)

- **China slowdown intensifying** — Factory activity contracted in July. Slowest GDP growth since 2022. Stimulus calls growing. China's teapot refiners ramping up Iranian oil buying, exploiting discounted crude. Imports bounced ~1M bpd in July from June's low but remain far below 5-year average. (Jul-Aug 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/economy/, https://oilprice.com/)

## Theses and hypotheses

- **The diesel crisis is a structural shift, not a cyclical blip** — The observation: diesel crack spreads at record highs, Russian exports at multiyear lows, global refinery throughputs ~5M bpd below year-ago, seaborne product trade down 3.8M bpd. The thesis: this is not just a Hormuz or Ukraine effect — it's the convergence of Middle Eastern and Russian refinery disruptions, aging refining capacity, and demand that's been structurally underestimated. Even if Hormuz reopens, the refinery damage in Russia is slow to repair, and the global refining system is running out of slack. Diesel will stay expensive relative to crude for years, not months. (Observation: Aug 2026 diesel reporting, https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Russias-Diesel-Exports-Crash-to-Multiyear-Low-amid-Tight-Global-Market.html)

- **The Hormuz closure is reshaping global oil maps** — The observation: with Hormuz effectively closed, oil flows are rerouting. UAE is ramping exports via ADNOC spot tenders. India seeking spot crude. China's teapot refiners buying discounted Iranian oil. Russia's refinery crisis deepening. The thesis: even if Hormuz reopens, some of these new trade patterns will persist — the closure is accelerating a structural shift in who sells oil to whom, with long-term implications for OPEC pricing power and Middle East geopolitical leverage. (Observation: Aug 2026 Hormuz reporting, https://oilprice.com/)

- **U.S. fiscal position is the real constraint** — The observation: $1.17T in annual debt servicing on $39.9T debt, with markets pricing no rate cuts for five years. The thesis: the Fed's ability to cut rates in a recession is severely constrained by the fiscal picture. The next downturn will hit a government that can't afford to stimulate aggressively AND a central bank that can't cut rates without spiking bond yields. This is the setup for a genuine fiscal-monetary crisis if growth truly rolls over. (Observation: Aug 2026 deficit reporting, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/us-budget-deficit-surged-in-july-to-highest-level-since-march-2021.html)

- **European climate risk is now economic risk** — The observation: the Rhine drought is the latest in a series of climate-driven economic shocks (wildfires in southern Europe, heatwaves, nuclear plant shutdowns in Romania, Hungary, France). The thesis: water scarcity and extreme heat are no longer tail risks — they're recurring drags on European GDP, especially in Germany's industrial heartland. Companies are adapting (stockpiling, rail diversion) but freight costs are "going through the roof" and nuclear shutdowns are structural, not temporary. (Observation: Aug 2026 drought reporting, https://oilprice.com/, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/drought-rhine-danube-water-levels-economy.html)

- **The price-destruction paradox in oil markets** — The observation: IEA projects 1.6M bpd demand destruction this year due to high prices, while the physical supply deficit is 1.8M bpd this quarter. The thesis: the market is in a feedback loop where supply constraints keep prices high, which destroys demand, which eventually undermines the case for high prices — but the refinery damage and geopolitical constraints are slow-moving, so the adjustment happens in stages, not all at once. This means volatile, range-bound prices that can spike on headlines but gradually erode fundamentals underneath. The real risk isn't $200 oil — it's that the world learns to live with $90-100 oil and the demand destruction becomes permanent structural change. (Observation: Aug 2026 IEA/OPEC reporting, https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Oil-Prices-Fall-as-OPEC-and-IEA-Slash-2026-Demand-Outlooks.html)
