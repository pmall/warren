## Findings

### U.S. inflation: easing but still above target

July CPI rose 0.1% m/m, annual rate 3.4%. Core CPI +0.2% m/m, 2.5% y/y — both in line with consensus. Energy prices fell 1.5% in July following a 5.7% drop in June, but are still up 14.7% annually. Shelter accounted for two-thirds of headline increase. The annual rate is down 0.1pp from June.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/cpi-inflation-report-july-2026.html

July PPI was flat (0.0%), below the 0.2% forecast. Core PPI +0.2% vs 0.3% expected. Goods prices fell 0.7% (energy -3.1%, gasoline -5.7%). Annual headline PPI 4.7%, core 4.2%. Second consecutive month of benign PPI readings.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/wholesale-prices-were-flat-in-july-below-expectations-for-0point2percent-increase.html

### U.S. labor market: sudden deterioration

Nonfarm payrolls fell 23,000 in July (expected +83,000). May revised down to +63,000. Unemployment 4.1%, but participation rate fell to 61.4% — lowest since mid-1976 outside Covid. Employment-to-population ratio 58.9%, lowest since May 2014. Average hourly earnings +3.2% y/y, lowest since May 2021. Government jobs fell 53,000; private payrolls +30,000. Healthcare (the leading job creator) added only 22,000 vs 36,000 12-month average. Leisure and hospitality lost 40,000 (World Cup ending).
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/jobs-report-july-2026.html

### Fed: rate hike expectations pushed out

Fed voted 9-3 to hold rates at 3.50-3.75% in July. Three dissenters wanted a hike. Before the jobs report, markets priced heavy odds for a September hike. After the jobs report + benign CPI/PPI, September hike odds dropped to 42%. Markets now pricing October or December as more likely for first move. Kevin Warsh (new Fed chair since May) had previously argued AI would be a significant disinflationary force, but has adopted a more cautious tone, noting "precise timing and magnitude of effects on the supply side remain hard to predict."
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/cpi-inflation-report-july-2026.html
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/wholesale-prices-were-flat-in-july-below-expectations-for-0point2percent-increase.html

### AI buildout: inflationary before it's deflationary

U.S. AI capital expenditure expected to reach $581 billion in 2026 (1.8% of GDP), rising to 2.8% by 2028 per Goldman Sachs. Only 17-20% of U.S. businesses report using AI (Census Bureau). Household electricity prices rose 10% over two years, faster than overall 6.2% CPI. DRAM costs up 400% from 2024 levels by end of year (JPMorgan). Computer software/accessories CPI +22.4% since July 2024. Minneapolis Fed's Kashkari dissented specifically citing AI data center demand as adding to inflation. Fed Chair Warsh appointed Stanford's Charles Jones and Marc Andreessen to a task force on AI's economic effects.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/ais-costly-buildout-complicates-the-feds-inflation-fight.html

### Iran war / Hormuz: the dominant risk

U.S.-Israel war with Iran ongoing. Strait of Hormuz effectively closed. Ship traffic at weekly lows. Iran says Hormuz will stay closed until U.S. meets its conditions. Tanker data contradicts U.S. claim that Middle East oil flows have normalized. IEA: global oil deficit to hit 1.8 million bpd this quarter. EIA: 600,000 bpd of Middle East oil could remain offline through end of 2027. Oil prices: WTI ~$81, Brent ~$87. OPEC and IEA both slashed 2026 demand outlooks. Hormuz stalemate raises risk of $120 oil per BofA.
Source: https://oilprice.com/

### U.S. budget deficit: surging

July deficit $432.3 billion, up 48% y/y, highest since March 2021. FY2026 YTD deficit ~$1.8 trillion, surpassing same period in 2025. Medicare expenses $174B in July alone (up from $103B in June). Debt financing $1.17 trillion YTD on $39.9 trillion national debt. Net interest $931 billion. $33 billion in tariff refunds (Supreme Court ruled certain levies illegal). Markets not pricing in any rate cut for next five years.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/12/us-budget-deficit-surged-in-july-to-highest-level-since-march-2021.html

### UK economy: resilient but threatened

UK grew 0.4% in Q2 (0.6% in Q1), on track for strongest G7 growth for second straight quarter. Annualized H1 growth ~2%. Business investment +1.7% (expected -0.5%). Hot weather + World Cup helped. But Treasury officials briefed PM Andy Burnham that growth could slow to 0.3% next year if Hormuz disruption persists. UK highly exposed to energy prices due to oil/gas imports.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/iran-war-g7s-fastest-growing-economy.html

### Europe drought: economic damage

Rhine water levels at Kaub fell to 24cm, lowest since records began in 1880. Critical navigation threshold is 78cm. Could dampen German GDP by up to 0.2% in Q3 (~€1-2 billion loss). Romania shut down its sole working nuclear reactor cooled by the Danube; deployed naval forces to blast rocks in riverbed to improve water flow. Hungary's Paks nuclear plant (40% of country's electricity) threatened. Serbia cut hydropower. France power prices jumped 22% as heatwave trimmed nuclear output. Global gas turbine orders hit record high as power demand surges.
Source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/drought-rhine-danube-water-levels-economy.html
Source: https://oilprice.com/

### Ukraine-Russia: energy infrastructure escalation

Ukraine struck Gazprom's 200,000-bpd Salavat refinery in the Urals. Ukrainian drone/missile strike on Novorossiysk damaged grain export terminals (last major Russian naval base on Black Sea). Russia's diesel exports crashed to 80,000 bpd in early August — multiyear low. Russia importing Indian fuel as refinery crisis deepens. Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries + Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure deepening global diesel supply crunch.
Source: https://oilprice.com/

China's teapot refiners poised to ramp up Iranian oil buying. High oil prices delivering windfall for China's coal-to-chemicals industry.
