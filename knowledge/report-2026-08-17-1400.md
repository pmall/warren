## Trump threatens to bomb Oman as ceasefire expires

Trump said informal talks are happening with Iran's Revolutionary Guard but insisted the U.S. is in no rush: "They're good poker players, but they're dying. I have no time schedule. ## Hormuz traffic grinds to a halt

Only five commodity vessels passed the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, and none were registered to transit on Sunday (Kpler data via Reuters). Compare with 31 vessels the previous weekend. Shipping is down 90% since before the war began Feb 28 — the Strait normally carries ~130 vessel transits per day. Tankers moving with transponders off ("dark mode") are keeping a lid on prices but also obscuring the true flow picture. HSBC's Parash Jain: "chaos is the norm" for the industry now. (Aug 17, https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/Hormuz-Tanker-Traffic-Slows-to-a-Trickle.html)

## China oil imports jump 22% despite weak economic data

China's July crude imports rebounded 22% from June's decade-low to 8.45M bpd, as Beijing stockpiled at lower prices. But the economic backdrop deteriorated: retail sales +0.6% y/y (vs +1.5% expected), industrial output +4.5% (vs +4.8% expected), both slowing from June. Q2 GDP grew just 4.3%, the slowest since 2022. China's role as the "swing buyer" is the key variable — when it returns to international markets in force, oil bears will be in trouble. (Aug 17, https://oilprice.com/Latest-Energy-News/World-News/China-Oil-Imports-Jump-22-Even-as-Economic-Data-Disappoints.html)

## European gas storage crunch deepens

Europe heading into winter with gas inventories at a 17-year low. Supply tighter than 2022. EU ban on Russian LNG imports takes effect Jan 2027, but EU purchases of Russian LNG hit an all-time high this year (Belgium sourced 100% of gas from Russia in July). Summer demand higher than usual due to heatwaves. Competition with Asia for LNG intensifying. Governments may force traders to buy now, triggering a bidding war. Gas prices twice pre-war levels. (Aug 16, https://oilprice.com/Energy/Natural-Gas/Europes-Gas-Storage-Crunch-Deepens-Ahead-of-Heating-Season.html)

## Asian refiners seek alternative Saudi crude pickup

At least two Asia-based refiners asked Saudi Aramco to pick up cargoes at Egypt's Mediterranean port of Sidi Kerir instead of Yanbu on the Red Sea, due to Houthi threats at Bab el-Mandeb. Aramco has been shuttling crude from Yanbu to Sidi Kerir via the SUMED pipeline. Houthis allowing China-linked vessels through the Red Sea. Some refiners may scrap monthly term allocations entirely due to higher shipping costs via Mediterranean/Africa route.
