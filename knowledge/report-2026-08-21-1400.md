## Hormuz: Iran's grip weakens as 80% of vessels shift to Omani corridor

The most significant development since Aug 20 is the data showing Iran is losing practical control over Hormuz routing. Maritime data firm Kpler reports that over the past two weeks, more than 80% of liquids traffic has used the Omani-side route (a UN-authorised channel Iran opposes) or "dark" transits (AIS off). A month earlier, almost none used that route. Kpler analysts say Iran has "at least partially lost control."

CNN reported on Aug 19 that "Iran has lost significant control of the Strait of Hormuz," citing US naval patrols enabling alternative routing. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright cited combined transits (through the strait + reroutes) around 15 million barrels per day in a recent week — closer to the pre-war ~20 million bpd average than earlier depressed figures. However, traditional AIS-based tracking still shows very low visible daily vessel counts (often single digits vs. ~130 pre-war), partly due to dark sailings and ship-to-ship transfers.

Gulf News (Aug 21) reported the shift is accelerating: more tankers are navigating via the Omani side under US military protection, reducing exposure to Iranian-controlled areas. The Policy Centre for the New South (Morocco) noted the Hormuz crisis shows the global political economy "fragmenting" as energy markets reroute.

Key tension: US officials boast of covert escort operations moving 10-20 million bpd some nights, while Iran insists the strait "has been, is, and will remain Iranian." VP JD Vance said Iran no longer holds energy crisis leverage. Brent at $93.55 suggests markets remain skeptical despite the optimistic US narrative.

The MOU expired Aug 17 with no deal. Trump pivoted to "economic D-Day" on Aug 19-20. Iran's FM says Tehran only interested in deal to fully end war, not ceasefire. UKMTO logged vessel struck by projectile Aug 19. Crossings remain far below pre-war levels.
