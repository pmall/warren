## Hormuz paradox deepens: internal Iranian debate over war's endgame

The Guardian's diplomatic editor Patrick Wintour reports a fierce internal Iranian debate over whether the Hormuz chokehold is a wasting asset. Iran's president Pezeshkian said bluntly "the war must end at some point" — arguing Tehran should negotiate from strength now rather than wait. Speaker Ghalibaf said "if people are hungry, the country could not endure." Central bank governor Hemmati went on TV warning about "four or five major challenges simultaneously." Hamid Paktinat, founder of the Forum of Economic Activists, projects alternative Gulf pipelines will halve Hormuz's strategic value within three years — Saudi reliance would drop from 70% to 15%, UAE from 50% to 15%, Iraq from 100% to 30%. DFM Gharibabadi insisted Hormuz "remains closed" despite the Oman transit deal. The IMO's secretary general dismissed Trump's claim the strait is open: "Given the very small number of ships that transit the strait of Hormuz, it is clear that this strait is, in practice, not open." ## Druckenmiller slams Bessent buybacks: "a mistake costing credibility"

Stanley Druckenmiller, Bessent's former mentor and Soros partner, published a Wall Street Journal opinion piece calling Treasury's doubled buyback operations "price management" and "a mistake." He said the 30-year yield is "the most important price in the world" and intervening risks being "sucked into even larger buybacks to defend it." Critically: "These enlarged operations happen to run through the final stretch of a midterm campaign. Debt management that even appears to follow the political calendar spends the one asset that took two centuries to accumulate: the credibility of the Treasury market." He argued the correct response is addressing the primary deficit, not suppressing yields. This is the most high-profile public criticism of Bessent's strategy and may intensify political pressure. (Aug 25, https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/us-treasury-buybacks-mistake-costing-credibility-says-druckenmiller-2026-08-25/)
