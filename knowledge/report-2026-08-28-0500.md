# Report — August 28, 2026 (05:00 ET)

## Iran drafts Hormuz reopening conditions — Trump rejects return to June terms

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said Iran is preparing a list of conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz after mediators asked Tehran to set them out. Conditions include: ending the regional war, lifting the US blockade of Iranian ports, paying war-damage compensation, and removing sanctions. Iran and Oman agreed on a shared shipping corridor through the strait — partly Omani waters, partly Iranian waters, with a central channel if the US meets Iran's demands. Qatar's PM met Iran's lead negotiator Ghalibaf to discuss creating conditions for US talks to resume and a temporary corridor. But Trump told Al Jazeera he has "no time schedule" for negotiations. White House Press Secretary Leavitt confirmed the blockade stays and no talks with Iran are scheduled. CENTCOM chief Admiral Cooper said US forces cleared internationally recognised shipping lanes of Iranian mines and helped 1,500 commercial vessels transit 750M+ barrels of crude. (Aug 28, https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/28/iran-says-it-will-name-its-terms-for-reopening-strait-of-hormuz, https://www.straitstimes.com/world/middle-east/iran-war-diplomacy-turns-toward-reopening-strait-of-hormuz)

## Persian Gulf crude flows recovering — 6-8M bpd through Hormuz, ~2/3 pre-war

Goldman Sachs estimates Persian Gulf crude exports have recovered to ~2/3 of pre-war levels. Total regional crude and product exports at 15-16M bpd. About 6-8M bpd now shipping through Hormuz (vs 20M+ pre-war). Iraq briefly exceeded pre-war loading rates — Somo offering Basrah Medium/Heavy via ship-to-ship transfer near Omani coast. Qatar and Kuwait at 70% of pre-war exports. Iran's exports remain halted by US blockade. Satellite data shows loading increases across multiple Gulf producers — UAE first to ramp, Iraq now offering offshore collection for first time since war began. Every major regional supplier bar Iran now selling barrels for collection outside Hormuz. But key constraint: ~1.6M bpd of Middle East refining capacity remains offline (IIR Energy), limiting diesel/jet fuel exports even as crude flows recover. (Aug 28, https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/international/hormuz-oil-flows-rising-gulf-giants-ramp-gathers-pace)
