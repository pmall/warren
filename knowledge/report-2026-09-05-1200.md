## Iran-Hormuz: Renewed strikes end monthlong lull, narrative war intensifies

The monthlong lull in US-Iran fighting ended with a renewed exchange of fire Sep 1-4. CENTCOM hit Iranian targets including two ships. Iran struck US bases in Kuwait (Ahmed Al Jaber) and UAE (Al Minhad) with missiles/drones in the 31st phase of Operation Saeqeh Sep 3. Iran struck at least 13 commercial vessels in August — the most recent, MT Sidr (Sep 2), killed 2 Filipino seafarers. Kuwait called it a "direct threat." Iran claimed new naval mines south of the strait.

The narrative war is as important as the kinetic one. JMIC data (using US NCAGS data) shows single-digit transits in both directions, while US NCAGS claims traffic flows averaging over 20 vessel transits per day for the last week. The Trump administration claims 30-40 ships transiting daily. Al Jazeera's Sep 3 investigation detailed why "data doesn't match US claims." Iran's VP Aref warned of "dark months" for the US economy. VP Vance said "I wouldn't call it a war" — even as Iran fired at Kuwait. EU formally joined US-led sanctions on Iran Sep 4.

Key: Iran may be escalating because its control over the strait is slipping — US boasted about growing numbers, and Iran risks conceding leverage if it doesn't respond. Pattern: when narrative shifts against Iran's control, Iran escalates. A record 17M barrels transited Hormuz on Monday Sep 1, but this was likely a one-off escort surge.
