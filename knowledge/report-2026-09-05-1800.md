## Iran-Hormuz: US strikes tanker near Kharg Island, Iran re-closes strait over Lebanon

The biggest escalation of the day: an Iranian tanker was hit by four US missiles near Kharg Island — Iran's main crude export terminal handling ~90% of its exports — on Saturday Sep 5. Tasnim and IRIB confirmed the strike at 8am local time; crew evacuated, no casualties reported. Kharg's loading capacity is 7M bpd with 30M barrels storage. Trump had threatened to strike Iran's fortified Pickaxe Mountain "very soon" the day before. This is a direct hit on Iranian oil infrastructure, not just military targets.

Separately, Iran's military announced it has re-closed the Strait of Hormuz in response to Israel's renewed strikes on southern Lebanon, citing a breach of the US-Iran ceasefire framework. IRGC Navy warned: "Do not approach the Strait of Hormuz; otherwise, your security will be jeopardized." CENTCOM responded that safe passage remains and claimed 55 merchant vessels transited on the day of the announcement — though this may reference pre-closure traffic. Only 4 visible commodity vessels crossed Hormuz on Thursday, well below the 10-day average of ~15. The narrative war continues: US claims 30-40 ships/day, Kpler/Lloyd's data shows single digits.

Iran International analysis: Iran's mine threat (rocket-launched mines from Larak Island) creates an asymmetric advantage — even if Iran can't deploy mines effectively, the *threat* alone deters shipping and forces the US into constant surveillance. Former CENTCOM commander Votel: "It puts a lot of pressure on us to make sure that we have constant surveillance."
